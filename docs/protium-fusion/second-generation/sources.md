# Primary sources and rate-update status ✨

Checked on 7–8 November 2026. Repository simulation records supply the pulse and apparatus comparisons. External literature supports specific reaction/conversion mechanisms and material methods; it supplies no observation of this reactor.

| Source | Evidence and use |
| --- | --- |
| [Robb et al., PRL 137, 162701, published 14 October 2026](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/tclf-b44m); [author full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20235) | New O16(p,γ)F17 measurements at 200–380 keV use activation and prompt gamma detection. The paper evaluates a new rate and discusses statistical/model choices. Read for the current oxygen-capture evidence; its complete rate table has not been substituted into this calculation. |
| [Iliadis, Palanivelrajan and de Souza, PRC 106, 055802 (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14354) | Bayesian evaluation of O16 proton capture, with roughly 4.2% published rate uncertainty. That uncertainty concerns the nuclear evaluation, rather than the delivered exposure or a reactor design. The newer 2026 paper uses different statistical/theoretical choices. |
| [Rapagnani et al., PRC 111, 025805 (2025)](https://journals.aps.org/prc/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevC.111.025805); [LUNA O17(p,γ) rate page](https://luna.lngs.infn.it/index.php/scientific-output/reaction-rate-repository?id=175&layout=edit) | Updated O17 destruction evaluation combines relevant resonance data and interference choices. The LUNA table page was read; its linked zip timed out during retrieval. Numerical replacement remains an explicit acceptance gate. |
| [Bruno et al., PRL 117, 142502 (2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.00483) | Direct measurement of the 64.5 keV O17(p,α) resonance gives a higher low-temperature return rate than older evaluations. It helps establish the return mechanism, with the 2025 evaluation taking precedence for future table integration. |
| [Gesuè et al., PRL 133, 052701 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03015) | First direct O17(p,γ) resonance-strength measurement at 64.5 keV. Gamma/alpha branching is temperature dependent; no universal oxygen-stage conversion is inferred from a resonance ratio. |
| [Zhang et al., Nature 610, 656–660 (2022)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05230-x); [full JUNA experimental account (2024)](https://www.nst.sinap.ac.cn/article/doi/10.1007/s41365-024-01531-0) | F19(p,γ)Ne20 is a measured proton-capture breakout route. The later account reports a new 225.2 keV resonance and a larger gamma/alpha ratio around 0.14 GK. Applying that enhancement uniformly at 0.5–1.7 GK would be unsupported. Its new table is not silently assigned to the frozen REACLIB calculation. |
| [Tan et al., O15(α,γ)Ne19 breakout measurement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17677960/) | Experimental nuclear information constrains a hot-CNO breakout route. The reactor calculation uses actual pulse alpha abundances and residence times; a stellar breakout label alone does not imply a large loss here. |
| [Barr and Moir, direct-converter tests (1983), author-hosted full paper](https://www.ralphmoir.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/fusion-science-and-technology.pdf) | Tests reached 100 kV and 6 kW. The reported TMX case recovered 79 W from 138 W incident and spent 12 W on suppression, or 48% net. Gas ionization, secondary electrons and breakdown were material limitations. This anchors the mechanism and local scales, rather than the proposed total power or efficiency. |
| [Mima et al., Fusion Technology 22, 56–65 (1992)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.13182/FST92-A30054) | Inductive/direct recovery analysis motivates an expanding-plasma pickup branch. Publisher metadata was accessible; full-paper retrieval was unsuccessful. The historical greater-than-80% modeled result is inherited from the earlier repository review, and is not treated as measured performance. |
| [Togo et al., Plasma and Fusion Research 10, 3405013 (2015), full paper](https://www.jspf.or.jp/PFR/PDF/pfr2015_10-3405013.pdf) | Traveling-wave deceleration experiment with a simulation beam supports phase-space-controlled deceleration, with modulation/acceptance still consequential. It does not qualify a mixed, quasi-neutral CNO plume. |
| [NASA, Brayton conversion modeling and mass study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20050244475/downloads/20050244475.pdf) | The inherited heat-engine method accounts for finite heat transfer, pressure loss and component mass. Retrieval timed out this run; its previous repository review and the explicitly reproduced cycle equations are distinguished from newly inspected papers. |
