# Plant integration and the 2049 history reassessment ✨

This memo reopens the selected history. The earlier Q=2.6 and two net-consuming pilots are authorial choices, rather than independent evidence against success. Equally, a successful power station needs a complete energy and material circuit; the request for a deployment does not establish one. The strongest conclusion is that **technical success below 1 mW/kg, with limited commercial deployment, is a credible branch**. The present calculations do not determine a commissioning date.

## Which resource constraints actually matter

The [capabilities review](capabilities.md) documents mature APM, replicating factories, about 0.9 solar luminosities of captured power by 2035, and 4.87×10^24 kg of Venus reclaimed in 2039–2046. Those capabilities support ambitious parallel experiments and specialized factories. They do not imply that an arbitrary plasma state or component efficiency can be manufactured.

The 1.466×10^15 kg ion reference uses **3.01×10^-10 of Venus's mass**. Its assigned 100 MJ/kg construction work equals **0.425 milliseconds of the swarm's captured output**. Its 1.261×10^21 J magnetic field could be charged at 95% efficiency in **15.36 days at 1 PW**. These energy equivalents are not delivery or construction schedules. They show that thousand-year repayment and large stored energy do not make this plant unaffordable to the established civilization. Specialized production, transport, high-field qualification, commissioning and component availability provide the relevant elapsed time.

The old opaque allocation is a different industrial proposition. Its 6.789×10^25 kg equals **13.95 Venus masses**. At the existing economic report's escape-work coefficients, lifting all that mass requires approximately **11.1 years of the entire 0.9-Lsun supply from Jupiter**, or **1,191 years from the Sun**, before transport, fabrication and losses. Manufacturing at 100 MJ/kg adds another 0.624 solar-supply years. Thus the statement that reclaimed Venus and the established small starlifting trial supply this full thermal enclosure by 2049 is not supported. It would require a newly selected major material campaign. The raw hydrogen/helium supply also cannot be silently treated as manufactured carbon.

This sourcing conclusion applies to the **old allocation**, not every opaque reactor. Its 10,000 kg/m² absorber and 10^25 kg driver are allowances, not demonstrated minimum masses. The saved carbon conduction constants give a diffusion scale about 0.22 m in 1,000 s, corresponding to roughly 450 kg/m² of carbon. That observation motivates a finite-thickness collector calculation; it does not establish its heat-storage, stopping, support or lifetime requirements. Delayed decay and annihilation photons also need a separate absorption calculation: [NIST graphite coefficients](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/XrayMassCoef/ElemTab/z06.html) give a mass-energy-absorption scale near 0.003 m²/kg at 511 keV gives an optical scale of only order unity through several hundred kg/m². Scattered buildup and actual geometry prevent treating this as a final leakage fraction. A thin catcher must include any lost photon energy in its electrical ledger. A magnetic catcher near 10^24 kg would instead require about one fifth of Venus and a substantial but possible transport campaign. Its interception, field topology, neutral-particle handling, coil impulse, converter and maintenance budgets must be designed. Field-energy volume and a virial mass estimate alone do not close that design.

## Fundamental consistency versus incomplete engineering

No fundamental contradiction has been established in the complete **accounting** of either reference. The opaque reaction cycle conserves baryons and energy; the relevant hot states have strong photon-thermalization margins. The ion model explicitly carries nuclear side reactions, potential circulation, conversion losses, structural mass, waste heat and replacement energy. Its magnetic support allocation does not obviously violate the virial relation: circumferential tensile stress contributes 2U_B to the integrated stress trace, while the required axial compression contributes approximately −U_B, leaving the field-energy scale U_B. Finite topology, strain compatibility and load transfer remain engineering requirements.

Several strengthened claims would be inconsistent:

- A bare rigid spherical opaque shell supported only by material strength is not the existing active-support model. The support must carry the specified gravity and momentum loads.
- A 30 K radiator at 80 AU does not automatically see a 3 K environment. For a black spherical collector under unobstructed sunlight, absorbed solar power is 129% of its 30 K emission capacity at 80 AU and 57% at 120 AU. Swarm occlusion, waste radiation, orientation, selective surfaces or shades need a site budget. A projected shade area is 5.20×10^13 m² for the reference radiator, before deployment supports; assigning 1 kg/m² would add 5.20×10^13 kg, not a prohibitive planetary mass.
- A small diagnostic collector cannot establish positive complete-cycle electricity while most pulse energy escapes. At the updated full-cycle H=24.71663 and U=2.118794 MeV per net helium, the simple 30%-driver/40%-thermal comparison requires captured fraction above **65.80%** before auxiliaries. At 99% capture it returns 1.505 times the driver source electricity. For the smaller N14 pulse with Q=8.182, capture must exceed **90.76%** at those efficiencies. These thresholds change with the converter and driver; they are not universal limits.
- Resetting the ion plasma does not require discharging the main solenoid. Conversely, a long reset interval does not replace continuous isotope processing when the primary Ti consumption time is about 302 s.
- A stationary 10,000-year average mass-replacement interval does not establish a 10,000-year irradiated surface lifetime. The budget is mass-weighted, and surface layers can be renewed separately.

The main unknowns are therefore coupled physical implementations, rather than a violated global conservation law. The ion route needs selective product removal, lepton management, high-field apparatus and recovery at the same operating point. The opaque route needs a deliverable low-entropy inflow, a useful sequence of stages, adequate interception, catalyst return and a receiver that survives repeated pulses. These are material engineering successes that a history may select after qualification. Our unfinished simulation cannot itself be the historical reason they failed.

## A less demanding ion heat system remains net positive

The 70%-conversion/30-K reference should not be treated as indispensable. I reran the saved source family at 60% thermal conversion and two radiator temperatures. This is a bounded architecture check, not a new source search or complete design.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 60% | 30 K | 1.12149 TW | 1.35534×10^15 kg | 0.82746 mW/kg |
| 60% | 50 K | **1.15976 TW** | **1.23283×10^15 kg** | **0.94074 mW/kg** |

The 50 K case retains the existing 140 T field, material properties, neutron capture and conversion assumptions. It reduces radiator mass from 1.404×10^14 to 1.808×10^13 kg, while refrigeration increases from 0.791 to 1.405 GW. Lower specific power does not automatically relax the 16 GPa hoop material, 8 GPa compression material, 1 TPa modulus, 140 T conductor or continuous particle-handling requirements.

At an assigned engine performance of 85% of internal Carnot, the 60%/50-K case can use internal temperatures 239.99/70.58 K. Minimum total exchanger conductance is 3.052×10^11 W/K. Reusing the explicit central helium-loop screen gives **2.320×10^13 kg** of fluid and pressure walls and **12.11 GW** pumping. Its equivalent radiator radius is 1,199 km; the idealized single supply/return pair has a 2,399 km total path and a 3.32 km bore. Real branching, manifolds, heat exchangers and deployment remain to be added. This calculation shows why the former near-30-K routing penalty need not dominate every architecture.

To expose a useful acceptance envelope, I additionally charged **10^14 kg and 100 GW**, beyond all existing allowances, for new integration. These are selected reserves, not measured equipment requirements. They comfortably exceed the idealized pipe-only screen but do not prove that all missing equipment fits. I then added electrical processing burden per primary event, including its converter capacity and radiator/maintenance feedback through the original plant functions.

Net zero occurs at **2.6501 MeV/primary** in this fixed family and allocation. The corresponding original 70%/30-K reference tolerates approximately 3.5604 MeV/primary before net zero, versus 1.1171 MeV/primary to retain 1 mW/kg. These are quantitative budgets for a specific extraction/clearing design. Choosing the row does not make that design real. The ion-dynamics reassessment should determine which burden and apparatus are physically coherent; reset, RF, heater and waste-heat credits must be counted once.

This makes 0.2–0.5 mW/kg a reasonable **conditional service envelope** if the specialized ion circuit meets the retained material assumptions and limits its added costs to the stated range. It is not an estimate of what will happen in 2049. It establishes that missing the preferred 1 mW/kg target can coexist with useful net output, rather than implying that a pilot must import electricity forever.

## Relative importance of integration problems

**Particle handling and field/material qualification are first-order ion requirements.** The reference processes about a petawatt of coherent energy to export terawatts. Small fractional losses therefore matter. A 1–2 MeV additional burden can use much of the net surplus, while still leaving positive operation in suitable cases. Shorter independently controlled cells, staggered resets, local pumping, selected low-absorption coatings and a different electrode/recovery arrangement are concrete approaches to test. None automatically grants lossless extraction or recovery of an isotropic thermal distribution.

**Heat routing is an important architecture choice, not the demonstrated universal obstruction.** The 70%/30-K comparison costs order 10^14 kg and tens to hundreds of gigawatts for its selected layouts. Increasing the radiator temperature and accepting 60% conversion substantially eases that geometry. Central and distributed engines are both live options. The old model's emitting-area allowance cannot substitute for pipes and exchangers, but neither should hardware already covered by allowances be charged twice.

**Radiation damage needs calculation; its severity should not be invented from the total terawatt rating.** The ion source has 4.216×10^12 m² of channel inner surface. Primary neutron production divided by this area is 3.125×10^19 m^-2 per year, and total nuclear heat divided by the area is only 2.55 W/m². These are distributed-source scale indicators, not local flux or damage predictions. Scattering, spectral displacement yields, electrostatic impacts and end streaming matter. The prescribed coil leakage similarly gives a mean deposited-radiation/conductor-mass ratio of about 594 J/kg/year; a mean dose does not establish the actual deposition profile or conductor lifetime. These scales provide no basis to assume catastrophic annual whole-plant replacement without a transport result.

At the reference replacement budget, 1.466×10^11 kg/year is available. Replacing every carbon channel wall would consume that budget once per 41.4 years; replacing the conductor would consume it once per 16.1 years, if each were the sole replacement. A 1 μm Ti30Zr30V40 liner weighs 2.438×10^10 kg, or 16.6% of one year's replacement allocation. Coating renewal and rare bulk-structure replacement are therefore distinct possible service contracts. The common-flux capture calculation nevertheless constrains that alloy to about 0.935 μm if the original 99.5% capture is to remain available before other losses. Conductive carbon, Zr-rich coatings or isotope-specified dopants offer physically named alternatives; resistivity, adhesion, outgassing and damage must be qualified together.

At unchanged source output, the original ion plant reaches maintenance net zero near **0.05424% of whole mass per year** at 100 MJ/kg, including its radiator/factory/spare feedback. Thus the reference can survive some increase above its assigned 0.01%/year, but not an arbitrary lifetime penalty. Repair energy per kilogram can also differ among surfaces, fluid, electronics and structure; the uniform 100 MJ/kg is a scenario rather than a physical constant.

**Startup is primarily a local power-handling and protection problem.** The magnetic energy is large compared with reactor export but small compared with established utility supply. Startup cannot be amortized by declaring it free, yet it need not postpone technical success for twenty output-years. A quench must route energy into appropriate external storage/dumps; dumping the entire field into the winding is not acceptable. The field remains on during ordinary plasma resets. The neutral V49 decay inventory gives a genuine roughly eleven-month recycling clock and requires initial catalyst stock, but experiments and parallel manufacturing can proceed during that interval.

**Opaque thermal surfaces may be locally renewed.** At one pulse every 5.46 days, renewing a 1 μm, 2,000 kg/m³ surface each pulse replaces about 1.34×10^-5 of a 10,000 kg/m² absorber per year. A 10 μm layer gives 1.34×10^-4. These are bookkeeping examples, not established stopping/erosion depths. They show why surface damage need not imply replacing the whole bulk collector, while thicker erosion could materially change upkeep. Recovering 99.99% of trace catalyst from those layers still needs a process design.

## Economics and the history selection

At 0.2–0.5 mW/kg and 100 MJ/kg, construction-energy repayment is about 15,800–6,340 years. Strong solar or utility competition near 80–120 AU makes widespread replacement of conventional energy infrastructure unlikely for these service points. The isotope comparison is also important: ordinary planetary deuterium reserves are not exhausted merely because the feed concentration is small. A working protium plant can instead be a research asset, a demonstration of independent fuel supply, a stationary refinery, or an option for long-lived dark-region infrastructure. Those benefits are choices about service and autonomy; they are not evidence that it sells cheap electricity.

The same economic logic permits a major catcher to be funded for experiments while commercial expansion is deferred. One should distinguish positive pulse gain, a closed catalytic sequence, positive complete-cycle electricity, complete-inventory specific power, and profitable deployment. A lower duty cycle can reduce opaque average thermal and recovery demands, but leaves much of its fixed collector mass and maintenance burden. It does not solve the old shell's construction problem by itself.

My recommended history boundary is:

1. Permit late-2040s **technical success** where a named mechanism and complete ledger support it. For the ion branch, a limited net-power installation below 1 mW/kg is a credible choice after qualifying particle selection/recovery and high-field modules. The exact output should follow that selected mechanism, using an envelope such as the one above.
2. Permit successful repeated opaque gain and linked catalytic stages without reusing Q=2.6 as a default derating. For a complete power plant by 2049, select and close a materially smaller catcher/driver architecture, with a concrete Venus or other resource allocation and transport history. Otherwise, a technically successful integrated research range followed by a deferred large collector is coherent. Deferral can be procurement rather than failed fusion physics.
3. Keep routine coating, pump and timing improvements as normal parallel engineering unless a specific coupled failure justifies a long interruption. Qualification campaigns, isotope turnover, radiation-damage observation and remote freight provide real clocks. Missing present simulations provide no such clock.
4. Preserve commercial restraint across successful branches unless a demonstrable reduction in capital, replacement or recovery costs changes the comparison. Huge civilization-wide resources can fund proof of operation without making the reactor a good source of further industrial growth.

This recommends a plausible branch and explicit acceptance conditions. It assigns neither a target-by-date requirement nor a probability that a particular remaining engineering capability succeeds.

## Reproduction of the bounded calculation

The new calculation calls `network_plant.plant` four times with the saved finite source family, `heat=.6`, `radiator_K=50`, and `extra_processing_MeV_per_primary` in `[0, 1, 2, 2.5]`. Every other nuclear, geometric and material parameter retains its default. Each result is passed to `phase-two/plant-audit/heat_transport.additional_cost_case` with `pipe_and_fluid_mass_kg=1e14`, `pump_electric_W=1e11`, and zero available fractions of old allowances. The generic cost keys here encode the selected integration reserve; they do not assert that it is all coolant hardware. An additional `heat=.6, radiator_K=30` call reproduces the saved 0.82746 mW/kg reference.

```python
import gzip, json, sys
from pathlib import Path
from network_plant import plant
p = Path('docs/protium-fusion')
sys.path.insert(0, str(p / 'phase-two/plant-audit'))
from heat_transport import thermal_power, exchanger, optimize_loop, additional_cost_case
energy = json.loads((p / 'network-ion-budget.json').read_text())
family = json.loads(gzip.decompress((p / 'network-channel-family.json.gz').read_bytes()))
for extra in [0., 1., 2., 2.5]:
    r = plant(energy, family, heat=.6, radiator_K=50.,
              extra_processing_MeV_per_primary=extra)
    z = additional_cost_case(r, {'pipe_and_fluid_mass_kg': 1e14,
                                'pump_electric_W': 1e11})
    print(extra, z['net_W'], z['total_mass_kg'], z['net_W_kg'])
    if extra == 0:
        h = thermal_power(r)
        x = exchanger(h['hot_input_W'], eta=.6, relative_Carnot=.85, Th=300., Tr=50.)
        print(x, optimize_loop(r, h, x, .5))
```

Run from the repository root with the existing NumPy/SciPy/pynucastro runtime and `docs/protium-fusion` on `PYTHONPATH`. The calculation writes no result files. Numerical mass-closure residuals in the 30/50-K cases were ±0.25 kg at 10^15 kg scale and energy residuals were zero. The underlying [plant audit](../phase-two/plant-audit/README.md) independently validates the hydraulic and mass-feedback equations. The new cases have not undergone a fresh physical component design.
