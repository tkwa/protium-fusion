# Protium fusion: reassessed 2049 history ✨

29 October 2026. This packet supersedes the **selected future outcomes** in [phase two](../phase-two/DEPLOYMENT-DECISIONS.md). Thomas asked us to reassess whether those failures represented a physical forecast or merely our present design readiness, and to write plausible actual histories for both designs. Successful operation was not a required premise. The resulting article selects successful specialist development with significant engineering changes and weak early economics.

The original history assigned a four-month liner interruption, a lost opaque campaign year and capture gain 2.6. Those were invented choices, not consequences of the simulations. They are withdrawn. The earlier numerical work remains useful evidence; its unresolved problems have not become solved real physics because the revised story contains successful engineers.

All new research and article edits were made in the attached `/home/tkwa/.codex/worktrees/protium-fusion/website` worktree. The other agents’ `/home/tkwa/src/website` checkout was left untouched.

## Judgment and selected history

The setting has superduperintelligence from 2029, advanced atomically precise manufacture, a Solar swarm capturing about 90% of the Sun by 2035, and Venus reclamation during 2039–2046. Against that background, a missing CFD solution or an ordinary contaminated liner is a weak reason to predict that an entire programme remains stalled in 2049. Engineering teams could vary geometry, pulse size, preparation, collection and operating point, and test them in parallel. Construction and qualification still take time. [Capability audit](capabilities.md).

My revised judgment favors useful late-2040s development over both programmes failing on the exact limitations of our first designs. The opaque route has the stronger constructive basis: several dense trajectories burn with substantial gain and retain radiation, and smaller stages permit a smaller complete installation. The ion route requires a more consequential control invention. Its selected success is defensible within this civilization, but is less firmly grounded in a computed working mechanism. Neither the year nor a probability of success was derived from a forecasting model.

## Oriel: the successful apparatus differs from the rejected one

The independent audit found that a single co-propagating monochromatic wave has an extremely narrow resonance acceptance. Its invariant `gamma − u_z` prevents it from cheaply collecting a warm distribution merely by assuming that 10% of particles enter each pulse. Raising amplitude, sweeping frequency or tiling pulses cannot retain the original cheap-fluence account automatically. This is an actual rejection of a specific implementation. [Ion mechanism memo](ion.md); [independent audit](cara-audit.md); reproducible [screen](ion-cara-screen.py) and [audit](cara_independent_audit.py).

The chosen future apparatus first uses nondegenerate capture: independently phased counterpropagating fields and a shaped magnetic guide form trapped packets, followed by a separately phased prepared-particle accelerator. Those fields can break the simple traveling-wave invariant. Preparation must export entropy and charge unsuccessful kicks, wall loss, uncaptured tails and repeated attempts. Physical cells about a kilometre long have local barriers and collectors; they are not just feed intervals in a continuous twenty-kilometre column. Maintaining the illustrative proton leakage requires roughly 4.8 MV more end barrier, with the extra end hardware and losses paid inside the selected reserves. Electron removal, heavy-product sorting and occasional residual-particle purges are included.

The selected measured **incremental electrical burden is 1.75 MeV per primary titanium event**, beyond the retained extraction, atomic-preparation, end-transfer and circulation accounts. It is not a cost calculated for the new capture stage. The exact conservative plant ledger gives 753.43 GW for this increment, 100 GW additional integration load, 424.62 GW replacement manufacture and 272.37 GW export. Complete mass is 1.3399976×10^15 kg. Fixed general auxiliaries remain fixed when the processing load rises; they are not silently reduced to improve the result. [Operating-point calculation and startup inventories](selected-ion-point.md).

The plant uses 60% heat conversion and 50 K radiators, with 10^14 kg additional integration mass. The full Solar luminosity incident at 80 AU is charged, including the possibility of swarm reradiation. Finite thermal transport, source-end equipment, isotope-specific neutron targets and the initial stable titanium bridge are allocated. The V49 half-life is about eleven months; it requires inventory and time to demonstrate equilibrium return, rather than a compulsory wait before any electricity can be produced. The bridge replaces the future decay inventory and is not counted twice.

The inherited 140 T nominal field, material stresses/current density, efficient potential recovery, neutron use and shielding remain demanding engineering requirements. A complete self-consistent birth-to-collection kinetic solution, finite magnetic layout, branched coolant system, irradiated-material lifetime and qualified nuclear transport have not been produced here. Their successful qualification is part of the selected future history. A later breakthrough would also be physically plausible; present computational incompleteness cannot distinguish exact dates.

## Moravec: smaller pulses, a complete factory and a different site

The reassessment replaces compulsory full-speed stream collisions with nearly co-moving local parcel assembly, graded packing and controlled compression waves. Neighboring parcels in a smooth converging flow need not dissipate their common inward velocity when they meet. Charging, neutralization, controlled ionization and weak-shock/ramp preparation provide concrete mechanisms. The previous cold-fluid surrogate omitted preparation energy and generated numerically uncontrolled entropy; it did not establish a gain ceiling. [Preparation assessment](opaque.md); [transport assessment](pulse.md).

Additional 64-zone, 27-isotope prescribed-hot calculations completed for small A, B1 and C13 pulses and a 3 m reference-scale helium factory. Prompt gains are approximately 8.18, 6.37 and 15.48 for the capture stages. The factory has very low carbon conversion and consumes net energy; it is included as a makeup process. Opacity diagnostics remain favorable in the burning material. These runs are constructive reduced-model examples, not engineered incoming-pellet demonstrations or multidimensional convergence studies. [Small-stage packet, statuses and reproduction](small-stages/README.md).

B2 releases substantial energy and reaches about 81.93% target conversion before a late numerical failure. Low-temperature rate extrapolations and an EOS table boundary were identified. The endpoint is near reaction freeze-out and all accepted shell velocities are outward, but its final expansion, shell interactions and receiving history are **incomplete**. Conditional cycle budgets use that endpoint openly. Neither the radiation-off sensitivity nor the temperature-floor attempt is promoted to a completed pulse. Qualified B2 continuation is an explicit future achievement.

The selected enclosed station uses a collector radius of 10^9 m, 60% electrical-to-prepared efficiency, 40% thermal conversion, 99% non-neutrino energy collection and catalyst return of 99.999% per pass. The isotope ledger includes catalyst makeup and delayed heat. Prompt radiation escaping the simulated plasma is included in collection before the single station-level energy-escape allowance; prepared driver energy is recovered once. [Complete station account](opaque-station.md); [independent plant audit](opaque-plant-audit.md).

The small local helium factory removes the old rare enormous storage pulse. Its lower carbon yield makes high catalyst return decisive. Electromagnetic slowing before material impact, secondary gas capture, offset ports and isotope sorting are the chosen processes. Return must include stopping transmutation and implantation, not only geometric escape. Container material, helium preparation, coating composition and irradiation performance also need qualification. The real calculations do not establish 99.999% return; that is a selected successful process result. At 99.99%, the chosen small factory and cadence perform much worse.

The most frequent stage repeats about every 24 minutes. About 3% of the saved B1 mass cannot use the simple full-radius launch schedule in the available clearance window. The story therefore gives the station shielded inner staging for the slow material, with release after the previous outflow passes. The actual revised trajectories, clearance and shielding are selected future qualifications within the large source/service allocation. The unmodified saved inflow cannot simply be repeated at the quoted cadence and called a demonstrated plant.

Whole mass is 10^24 kg, including the complete factory, two maximum electrical pulses in storage, processing inventories, guides, heat exchange, manufacture and roughly 3.19×10^23 kg of installed design reserve. These are engineering allocations, not a detailed bill of parts. Gross generation is about 3.20×10^21 W; the driver takes 1.71×10^21 W, replacement 3.17×10^20 W, and other auxiliaries/support their separate allowances, leaving 10^21 W. Full Solar absorption at 5 AU raises the required 330 K outer radiator coverage to about 80.84%, within the allocated surface and reserve. Peak power, storage, mean heat and mass identities were independently checked. Surface screens are conditional constant-property calculations; the selected operating temperatures and actual composite surfaces must be qualified together.

The 120 AU open range and 5 AU station are different facilities. Venus supplies structural stock; its atmosphere cannot supply the full carbon absorber. A major outer-planet methane-processing campaign supplies carbon. At an illustrative 3% recovered-carbon yield this means processing about 4.2×10^24 kg of feed, a substantial parallel industrial programme. No unestablished mass-removal rate is assigned to the small 2036 Solar starlifting trial.

At 30 km/s, the straight-distance cruise intervals are about 0.68 years from Venus to5 AU and2.37 years from20 to5 AU. The selected history uses powered convoys; these are kinematic scheduling scales, not solved ballistic Solar orbits. Solar gravity, angular momentum, acceleration and receiving operations require an actual transfer design and additional energy. The whole-project kinetic budget is 4.5×10^32 J; 50%-efficient launch takes 9×10^32 J, about 30 full-swarm days. Solar-orbit changes, carbon lift/processing and receiving/braking equipment remain additional. For scale, changing the circular orbital energy of the entire10^24 kg from Venus to5 AU would itself require about5.25×10^32 J of mechanical work; the actual mixed-source routing differs. This extra scale remains fundable in the stated multi-year programme and must not be hidden inside the kinetic injection allocation. Moving the first enclosed station closer avoids forcing the distant range's transport burden on the power plant.

## Economics and remaining uncertainty

At 100 MJ/kg fabrication, 1 mW/kg requires about 3,170 years to repay fabrication energy; Oriel's commissioning output requires about 15,590 years. These net outputs already deduct selected recurring replacement. Transport materially lengthens the opaque station's total investment repayment. A wealthy Solar civilization can afford strategic research with that poor payback, while solar collection and deuterium extraction remain better competitors for rapid replication and ordinary power markets.

These are selected first operating points, not universal mature specific-power ceilings. More efficient conversion, direct recovery, higher cadence, lower hardware mass, cheaper fabrication or different sites could change the economics. No cheap mass adoption is inferred from net generation. Later applications concern long-lived isolated stocks and deuterium depletion.

The strongest unresolved real questions remain coupled preparation/stability for opaque pulses, B2 cold-tail continuation, irradiation/stopping recovery, nondegenerate lepton capture and entropy removal, real field/thermal layouts, and experimentally qualified materials and transport. The new small-stage calculations and arithmetic audits narrow parts of that work. They do not establish what real-world systems will ultimately be built or when.
