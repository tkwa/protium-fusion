# Independent compact opaque-station audit ✨

The selected **10^24 kg / 10^21 W / 1 mW/kg** compact station has a consistent global energy and mass allocation. I reproduced its non-neutrino heat, electrical conversion, maintenance, support, pulse storage and radiator arithmetic independently in [opaque_station_audit.py](opaque_station_audit.py) and [opaque-station-audit.json](opaque-station-audit.json). Thirteen checks pass. These checks support a conditional future engineering boundary; they do not validate the prescribed inflow, incomplete B2 expansion or integrated components.

The corrected station counts prompt photons that left the modeled plasma as incident on the enclosing receiver and applies its 99% capture once. It gives 24.72216 MeV of nuclear heat and 3.58968 MeV of prepared input per net helium, with 5.22870 MeV of preauxiliary electricity at 60% driver and 40% thermal efficiency. Throughput is 1.78185×10^33 net helium/s. The independent global check equates nuclear input with useful export, rejected heat and escaped nuclear/prepared energy; recirculated input is recovered once.

Replacement uses 3.16881×10^20 W, support uses 2.65562×10^19 W, and total rejection is 5.97695×10^21 W. Mean incident collected heat is 636.76 W/m². A 330 K, emissivity-0.9 radiator covers 78.5887% of the outside sphere. Including the full central solar-luminosity envelope at the final **5 AU** site, with all outside surfaces absorbing and the radiator panels rejecting that load, increases this to **80.8374%**, adding 1.71028×10^20 W of absorbed external heat. The additional panels use 5.652×10^18 kg from the existing reserve, leaving 3.19377×10^23 kg; total installed mass, maintenance and net output remain fixed. If only the radiator panels absorb sunlight, coverage would be 80.3966%; the audit uses the more conservative whole-project budget. Directed beams or nearby hot facilities still need local siting rules.

Placing engines on the outside of the hot receiver avoids long coolant trunks. At a 1000 K reservoir, 330 K radiator, 40% conversion and a selected 80% of internal Carnot performance, the two-exchanger minimum-conductance screen gives internal temperatures 837.74/418.87 K and finite approaches 162.26/88.87 K. Total exchanger conductance per hot-input watt is 0.012914 K^-1. This leaves useful temperature differences for a practical cycle; the hardware implementing them belongs in the explicit local-manifold/exchanger reserve.

The two-pulse electrical buffer weighs 1.35813×10^21 kg at the assigned 100 kJ/kg installed energy. The largest pulse is B2 at 6.79066×10^25 J electrical input. Direct reconstruction from initial spatial profiles gives a largest kinetic-source peak of 4.41792×10^23 W for the 3 m helium factory, only 1.767 W/kg of the 2.5×10^23 kg fixed driver allocation. Summing all five stage peaks gives 4.856 W/kg. Internal-energy preparation, switching and overlap add ratings, but there is substantial allocated mass: **3.19383×10^23 kg remains as installed design reserve** after the named fixed and computed components. The point does not rely on apportioning a future shared factory to nonexistent customers.

The smaller factory is a real accounting improvement. B2 is explicitly incomplete. Its saved endpoint has colliding radial velocity shells, so the ballistic receiver routine rejects it. A scalar integrated-energy balance cannot settle its final arrival width or surface loading. Successful late expansion and a qualified heat pulse must therefore be selected as part of the future implementation, rather than described as outputs of the present numerical calculation.

Cadence requires a concrete source redesign. B1 recurs every **1433.90 seconds**, and 99% of its outgoing kinetic energy arrives by 332.00 seconds. This leaves about 1101.90 seconds for the next launch after that percentile of the previous outgoing energy has reached the receiver. The next shot's initial mass-flight quantiles are 465 s median, 920 s at 95%, 1588 s at 99%, and 3458 s at 99.9%. **3.05% of its mass has a longer flight than the available window; 1.29% has a longer flight than the full period.** These fractions are larger than a negligible central point. Inner staging, retimed loading, protected delivery or an altered central velocity profile must supply that material while preserving the required prepared state. Nor does the 99%-energy arrival percentile establish complete mass/gas clearance. The chronology should name this as a commissioning change and attribute successful operation to the revised, measured source.

Other load-bearing future achievements remain 99.999% catalyst return, including stopping-induced nuclear changes and implanted or escaped material; 60% net preparation efficiency with the selected entropy and symmetry; helium preparation whose packaging is carried through the composition ledger; and combined surface lifetime/renewal within the replacement allocation. Named approaches such as staged slowing, light-element receiving layers, secondary-gas recovery and isotope-resolved accounting make these concrete engineering programmes. Geometric enclosure alone grants none of those efficiencies.

Finally, the material programme must follow composition. A bulk carbon receiver of 1.257×10^23 kg cannot come from the approximately 10^20 kg of carbon in Venus's atmosphere. A separate outer-planet carbon campaign or a qualified carbon-faced composite absorber is needed. The station memo now identifies this sourcing work and separately accounts for transport. Its final 30 km/s Venus-to-5 AU cruise takes about 0.676 years; a 20-to-5 AU carbon delivery takes 2.37 years at that speed. Cruise kinetic energy for the 10^24 kg station is 4.5×10^32 J, and 50% launch efficiency costs 9×10^32 J, about 30.2 full-swarm days. Braking and source-specific transport still need their own equipment. This exceeds the assigned 10^32 J fabrication work and preserves the case for strategic funding rather than low-cost early electricity. The established swarm can nevertheless fund such a selected specialist installation if it allocates the specified material, power and transport programme.

No further universal obstruction emerged from this bounded full-plant audit. A late-2040s specialist success is a coherent selected history under these stated future achievements. Its specific output is an accounted engineering target, and its weak economic repayment supplies the reason for limited replication.
