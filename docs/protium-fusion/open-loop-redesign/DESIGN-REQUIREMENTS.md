# Open loop protium fusion design requirements ✨

30 October 2026. Thomas requested an opaque-system redesign supplied with external carbon, with the objective of finding the best supported net electrical power per installed kilogram. The fresh carbon-to-hydrogen supply target is **0.007 kg/kg**. This record treats that number as a mass ratio, as stated in the working conversation; it is a user-selected design resource budget rather than a measured universal elemental abundance.

The open material boundary admits fresh carbon to replace catalyst consumed in side reactions or lost in recovery. Catalyst recycling remains available where its equipment and energy costs improve performance. A local helium-to-carbon factory is removed from this branch. Hydrogen consumption means fresh supplied protium, including hydrogen lost with discarded material; gross recirculating proton flow must not be used as the denominator. Supply isotope fractions and any enrichment must be explicit.

Net useful power subtracts preparation, separation, recovery, receiving, control, support and recurring replacement. The installed mass includes the pulse source and its peak-power equipment, energy storage, receiver, shielding, heat conversion and rejection, isotope processing and decay inventories, support, internal feed buffers, manufacturing capacity and spare stock. Externally provided feed is the boundary condition; extraction and delivery costs are reported separately as sensitivities, with a clear distinction between supplied-feed and expanded system boundaries.

Use the corrected spherical hydrodynamic stages from the adjacent B2 completion work. Numerical completion requires final-time success, physically signed artificial dissipation, ordered cells, conservation checks, and tolerance or resolution comparisons. Source preparation and multidimensional stability remain separate from prescribed-inflow calculations.

The design search must couple pulse size and count, clearance and source timing, mean and transient receiver loading, radiator temperature and area, conversion efficiency, source and storage ratings, material recovery and carbon consumption. Existing fixed mass allowances are comparison points rather than a demonstrated optimum. Show which hardware requirements determine the best point in the tested family and distinguish them from physical bounds. Do not claim a global optimum from a finite or assumption-limited search.
