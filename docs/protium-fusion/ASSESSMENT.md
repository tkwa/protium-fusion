## Answer to the mass and power question

There is no basis here for declaring a Jupiter mass a universal lower limit. Stellar ignition criteria describe gravitationally confined objects with particular compositions and thermal histories. Artificial confinement, pulsed compression and separated catalytic reactions pose different constraints. Equally, a favorable nuclear reaction or an algebraically closed plant budget does not establish an achievable reactor.

The investigation produces two conditional allocations meeting the numerical target. Their strongest result is a quantitative set of requirements and sensitivities. Neither is a validated complete reactor, and the work does not establish which method will ultimately be used or a defensible real-world completion date.

## What the two approaches do about bremsstrahlung

**Opaque pulses.** Radiation emitted inside an optically thick hot region can be reabsorbed and remain part of its energy inventory. The calculation includes a nuclear network, radiation energy and pressure, an electron–positron equation of state and gray diffusion in a spherical flow. This changes the role of bremsstrahlung from immediate escape to transport and eventual heat recovery. It also makes radiation pressure, entropy, opacity and boundary leakage central to compression and gain. Opacity alone does not solve the driver problem.

The spatial model starts from a specified convergent flow. No demonstrated driver creates that state with the assumed efficiency and symmetry. The surrounding collector has a radius of 20 million kilometres; it requires active support, repeated debris recovery and heat rejection. The revised maintenance allocation needs 31.75% more pulse throughput than the earlier budget. See [RADIAL-MODEL.md](RADIAL-MODEL.md), [ENGINEERING-HYPOTHESES.md](ENGINEERING-HYPOTHESES.md), [PLANT-BUDGET.md](PLANT-BUDGET.md) and [ECONOMICS.md](ECONOMICS.md).

**Electron-poor ions.** Removing most electrons suppresses the usual electron–ion bremsstrahlung channel. The proposed Ti49(p,n)V49 reaction provides neutrons; cold electron capture returns titanium, neutron capture on hydrogen makes deuterium, and subsequent light-fuel reactions produce helium. The explicit network accounts for side products, recycling, small makeup streams, neutrinos and processing power. This avoids relying on a useful rate of direct proton–proton weak fusion. It requires maintaining a highly charged plasma and returning processed material.

Simple narrow-beam carbon variants fail the tested energy budget after angular recycling is included. The more favorable thermal titanium allocation instead has large magnetic and electrostatic energy circulation. Its assumed end transfer is 99.9%; reducing it to 99.8% lowers output to about 0.942 mW/kg. Halving the assigned hoop and axial working stresses lowers output to about 0.369 mW/kg. These are sensitivities, not probabilities or predictions of material progress. See [NONNEUTRAL-BEAMS.md](NONNEUTRAL-BEAMS.md), [CATALYST-RECYCLING.md](CATALYST-RECYCLING.md) and [NETWORK-PLANT.md](NETWORK-PLANT.md).

## What remains to establish in the ion branch

The reference has 1.117 MeV of extra electrical expenditure per primary titanium event before dropping below the requested specific power, with its existing mass allocation. Any added equipment also changes that margin.

The lepton work identifies restricted wall access, end-barrier difficulties for positrons, internal nuclear-pair scenarios and substantial differences between unmagnetized and strongly magnetized collision models. It does not yet provide a complete physical birth-to-collection balance. External photon conversion, residual gas, injection, charge exchange and finite collector capture still matter.

The latest transverse-field calculation separates bare single-particle spectra from a collective centroid approximation. Two approximate collective modes cross the proposed lepton drift sweep. Neither model supplies the self-consistent absorption and capture of an actual clearing field. Rotational work, recovered power and useful ion heating must be accounted together; treating every absorbed joule as an additional heater loss can double-count a load already present. The saved calculations provide controls and constraints for such a design, rather than a selected collector.

Radiation shielding and neutron capture also retain prescribed transmission and capture fractions. Spatial transport, competing absorptions, structural damage, conductor lifetime and isotope separation must be demonstrated within the plant's mass and power budgets. A 140 T field, the assumed current density and kilometre-scale working stresses have not been qualified together. The most useful next research is an actual extraction/collection arrangement and an integrated material/transport assessment. More orbit samples alone cannot close those gaps. ## Economics and timing

At 1 mW/kg, a gigawatt requires 1e12 kg of installed plant. Construction energies of 1, 10 and 100 MJ/kg imply simple energy paybacks of about 32, 317 and 3169 years. The ion reference, at its higher specific power, repays the 100 MJ/kg construction scenario plus initial field charging in approximately 2170 years. Its quoted net output already deducts replacement manufacture.

Deuterium extraction can access a much smaller fraction of a hydrogen reservoir through easier reactions. At the historical central Jovian abundance and an assigned 30% net conversion, 401 kg/s of hydrogen feed supplies enough deuterium for 2.160 TW. The conditional protium plant consumes 17.86 g/s of hydrogen. It accesses about 22,500 times more useful energy from the initial reservoir in this comparison, but that advantage becomes economically decisive only at an appropriate demand, depletion or access constraint.

Solar equipment assigned 30% net conversion and complete areal mass of 1 or 10 kg/m² reaches 1 mW/kg at 639 or 202 AU from a Sun-like star. These comparisons make long-lived infrastructure in dark regions, depleted feedstocks and possible stationary fuel manufacture plausible applications. They do not establish a market price or profitability. Accretion can offer higher energy yield per fuel mass, while requiring its own seed and installation; natural stellar black holes exceed the specified mass limit.

Better simulation can shorten design iterations and expose failure modes. Demonstrating the plasma state, qualifying materials, proving repeated component operation and constructing the installation remain separate milestones. There is no quantitative basis in the present evidence for assigning dates to those milestones. The article therefore reports early investigations and component experiments within its approved fictional history, while leaving later achievements open.
