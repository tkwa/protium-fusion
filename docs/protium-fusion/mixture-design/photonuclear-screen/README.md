# ✨ Photonuclear omission: bounded importance check

This supports the EM-only Geant4 receiver study. It does not replace coupled photon, charged-particle and neutron transport.

The [Edge1960 primary abstract](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.119.1643) reports C13(gamma,n) = 94.1 ± 10 **microbarns** for an effective 6.4 MeV mixed gamma source. Searchable ENSDF text displays “mb”; using that rendering would introduce a factor1000 error. [Green and Donahue1964](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.135.B701), Table II, gives 0.32 ± 0.04 mb at weighted 6.61 MeV and 0.4 ± 0.1 mb at 7.16 MeV. Its C13 data do not supply an exact 5.657 MeV measurement. The original table was visually checked in the [NBS carbon facsimile archive](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-C13-518f4d604594649e67f826fc87f6550e/pdf/GOVPUB-C13-518f4d604594649e67f826fc87f6550e.pdf), printed datasheet466, PDFpage475.

For natural graphite with assumed C13 number fraction0.0107, the nuclear coefficient is `kappa_nuc = N_A/A * f13 * sigma13`. Comparing it with the [NIST photoatomic attenuation coefficient](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/XrayMassCoef/ElemTab/z06.html) gives the first-interaction branch `P_first = kappa_nuc/(kappa_nuc + mu_EM)`. At the actual nearby measurement energies, this is approximately2.1e-6 and7.3e-6. Carrying0.32mb to5.657MeV as an explicit sensitivity gives6.75e-6; even a specified10mb sensitivity gives2.11e-4. Neither extrapolation is a measured point or rigorous bound. C13(gamma,n) produces useful C12, so the primary reaction is isotope conversion rather than terminal catalyst destruction. Subsequent neutron capture, leakage and isotope changes need their own ledger.

At20MeV, natural carbon's NIST photoatomic cross section is approximately314mb per atom. The original [Fultz1966 photoneutron data](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.143.790) are of order0.5mb at20MeV, and the [Carchon1976 photoproton curve](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevC.14.456) is a few mb there. These rough visual values were checked in NBS PDFpages189 and378, not treated as a precise digitized evaluation. C12(gamma,3alpha) and other open branches require inclusion in a total cross section.

The script therefore uses specified total-photonuclear sensitivities of5,10 and20mb at20MeV. Their first-interaction branches are1.57%,3.09% and5.99%. For the caller-supplied prompt share of1.406% of H+U, the products are0.0220%,0.0434% and0.0842% of H+U. These products indicate scale; repeated scattering, secondary photons, energy degradation and neutron transport prevent interpreting them as full energy-loss bounds. Nuclear absorption also does not imply that all photon energy escapes as neutrons.

For an accounting checkpoint that retains EM-only transport, assigning **all of the specified prompt energy to a reserve** is a defensible conservative treatment of its thermal contribution. Apply that reserve explicitly and only once. A1.406% reserve alone limits capture to98.594% even if every other joule is retained; retaining99% of the rest gives97.60806% total. It cannot be hidden inside an existing1% whole-energy loss allowance. Use the actual prompt fraction for each selected source family, since1.406% here is a scenario input supplied by the receiver study.

The reserve covers prompt-derived thermal accounting. It does not certify the terminal isotope-conversion budget or account for escaped neutral gas. A useful later calculation is to fold evaluated photonuclear coefficients over all above-threshold photon track lengths from the transport run, then transport produced neutrons. The particle and isotope outputs should distinguish C13-to-C12 recycling from C12-to-boron or helium conversion.
