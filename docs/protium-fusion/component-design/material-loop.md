# Material recovery and isotope processing ✨

The useful current architecture is a closed material loop with separate gas handling, retained solid fuel, isotope sorting and a held nuclear side-product stream. Material temporarily implanted in a catcher, left in a chemical residue or sent back through an isotope cascade remains station inventory. The available fresh-carbon supply is a ceiling; the process need not consume it.

## The revised accounting boundary

`material_cycle.py` reads actual input composition from `initial_Y`, `initial_mass_fractions`, an explicit composition or the first saved trace state. Stage names do not assign hydrogen content. Its catalyst balance is `(F − rC M) a = f`, where `F` contains each recipe's actual C12/C13/N14/N15 input and `M` its fully decayed output. It counts excess unburned hydrogen, separate eventual CNO/H returns, arbitrary other input/output species, explicit decay maps and atomic mass defects. Completed mixture runs are checked against their requested **physical duration**, rather than an invariant sound-time count. The legacy baseline, a nonidentity recipe-basis transformation, excess-H return and multiple-daughter decays pass 45 checks.

Here `rC` and `rH` mean eventual return after retained reservoirs and repeated processing. They do not mean first-pass extraction yield. `solve_supply_target()` returns a **maximum allowable unrecoverable-loss boundary**. Its old 99.96276% value follows from spending the whole .0056 fuel-carbon allowance; it is neither a predicted loss nor an apparatus requirement that such a loss occur.

With complete eventual ordinary-material return, the original four-stage pulse family requires **C:H = 0.000360705**, about **913 kg/s fresh carbon and 2.532 million kg/s fresh hydrogen** at the old helium-production rate. The earlier .0056 accounting spent about 14,194 kg/s carbon. The residual carbon demand comes almost entirely from conversion into O16 outside the selected four-stage routing. Approximately **1,216 kg/s O16** is held inside the station, adding **3.84×10¹⁰ kg per year**. The trace Ne/F/Na/Mg streams are much smaller. These are identified nuclear side products, rather than unobserved ordinary-matter escape. See `material-reservoir.json`.

The first actual H-richer comparison, A with two protons per N14 and B1 with two per N15, gives approximately **C:H = .000358667**, prepared energy **2.00867 MeV/net He** versus **2.29338** in the perfect-return original family, and lower gross fuel circulation. It increases unburned H from about **1.8581 to 3.8169 atoms per net He**. `material-cycle-A2-B1H2-comparison.json` records this illustrative combination; the parent's broader mixture grid selects the eventual operating recipe.

The subsequent provisional 003 mixture selection, A-H3/B1-H2/B2-H2.5/C13-H4, uses **C:H = .000358636** at complete eventual ordinary-material return. At 4.4734×10³² net He/s this is **1,074 kg/s fresh C**, **2.995 million kg/s fresh H**, **38.05 million kg/s reusable CNO**, and **4.00 million kg/s returned unburned H**. Its conditional export is 8.012×10²⁰ W. These numbers are a specific 64-zone comparison point, preserved in `material-plant-003.json`; further mixture or mesh changes should regenerate the material projection.

## A component sequence

1. **Capture and segregate the physical streams.** A supported catcher absorbs the pulse and retains implanted C/N. Hydrogen and helium which desorb promptly enter local capture channels, traps or a back-side plenum. The exact gas path remains coupled to catcher design. At the provisional A-H3/B1-H2/B2-H2.5/C13-H4 operating point, the separate uniform-cavity screen gives an H2+He column of **7.31×10⁻⁷ kg/m² at 1000 K** if all returned gas enters the cavity. For 1 cm radius grains at 2.4 million m/s and an illustrative 10 kJ/kg gas-heating allocation, chamber injection fraction divided by effective global capture probability must stay below about **0.0634**; 1 mm grains require ten times less. If effective global capture is 1% per wall encounter, local routing must prevent roughly 99.937% of the return gas from entering the cavity. See [return-gas.md](return-gas.md). This constrains gas residence and local plumbing, not eventual material return. Letting millions of kilograms per second reside in the central cavity would alter incoming-grain heating even if every atom were ultimately collected.
2. **Retain C/N-bearing skins for an economical batch interval.** A rotating population of receiver tiles or removable skins can remain exposed for months. A larger circulating isotope inventory supplies new pulses while their predecessors are still implanted. Prompt gas handling has its own much shorter residence time.
3. **Liberate the implanted material in a closed process cell.** Candidate routes include selective release from graphite; processing a thin refractory oxide skin; or chemical extraction from a contained metal catcher. Recover all volatile products and return unreacted solids to the process. An incomplete chemical conversion creates a retained reject stream.
4. **Separate chemical elements before isotopes.** Remove H/He, route C-bearing species and N-bearing species to distinct trains, and assay the trace O/F/Ne/Na/Mg fraction. Retain stage identity where convenient: B1 already makes predominantly C12 and N15, while the C13 stage makes principally C13 and N14. Mixing all pulse batches creates avoidable isotope-separation work.
5. **Use finite-purity isotope cuts with recycled tails.** Both sides of a C12/C13 or N14/N15 cut stay in the loop. Assayed off-specification material receives another pass. Properly counted excess H returns to fuel preparation; helium is a managed product stream.
6. **Return nuclei to the source recipe.** Source coatings, grain matrices and chemical carriers belong in actual input composition and in the material ledger. Carbon transferred from a recyclable structural skin into a fuel coating can be balanced internally; it is not automatically fresh carbon consumption.

This specifies functional stages and mass boundaries. The local catcher-to-plenum transfer, chemical liberation rate and final source conditioning still require component geometry.

## Residence time removes a false throughput constraint

For a per-pass extraction fraction `c` and zero irreversible loss, eventual return is one, the mean number of passes is `1/c`, and repeated rejects do not consume fresh catalyst. If an actual fraction `ell` irreversibly leaves on each pass, eventual return is `(1−ell)c / [1−(1−ell)(1−c)]`. The distinction is implemented and checked in `material_reservoir.py`.

Processing a new 10 µm graphite skin after every shot would handle roughly 4.6×10¹³ kg/s. That is an expensive optional architecture. Processing the same thickness annually handles about **9.64×10⁹ kg/s** and uses **1.93×10¹⁶ W at 2 MJ/kg**, roughly **0.003% of present net export**. A 50 µm alumina skin weighs 0.195 kg/m². Annual processing at 30 MJ/kg needs **0.185 W/m²**, roughly **0.43% of present net export**. These costs apply only if the specified whole skin is processed; selective liberation can differ.

The important local scale is modest: a 1 km² alumina tile processes about 195 tonnes of its 50 µm skin per year and collects of order 0.1 tonne of pulse material. The huge station-wide throughput follows from its huge area. With retained CNO circulation around 41 million kg/s, a one-year complete delay requires of order **1.3×10¹⁵ kg** of initial circulating CNO; a uniform distribution of tile ages holds about half that mean amount. This is around 10⁻⁸ of the station mass. It is a real startup isotope inventory, with synthesis and delivery requirements, but it is not a large denominator penalty. The exact stage inventories and discrete pulse buffers are assessed in `schedule.md`.

For a physically chosen full alumina-to-Al/O2 reduction step, NIST-JANAF gives a standard reduction free-energy inventory of **13.35 MJ/kg at 1000 K**; 50% conversion of supplied work into that free-energy change gives **26.70 MJ/kg**, before heat integration and handling. Complete reduction is one process route, not a necessary step in every oxide-skin regeneration. [NIST-JANAF alpha alumina](https://janaf.nist.gov/tables/Al-096.html).

## A renewable graphite isotope pool

Carbon recovery can draw equivalent C12 and C13 nuclei from an assayed, recyclable wall pool. Identifying and extracting the particular implanted carbon atoms is unnecessary. The equation for each isotope is `dW_i/dt = collected_i + fresh_i + structural_return_i − fuel_draw_i − structural_withdrawal_i`. Retained processing rejects remain inside `W_i`; different isotopes remain separately counted.

At the 003 point, the C12 fuel draw is **9.753 million kg/s**, collected C12 return is **9.752 million kg/s**, and fresh C12 is 1,062 kg/s. Corresponding C13 rates are **386,636 kg/s**, **386,623 kg/s**, and **12.44 kg/s**. Both pool equations close. An assumed naturally composed 50 µm graphite skin at density 2,000 kg/m³ contains **1.76×10¹⁶ kg C13**. That is startup wall inventory with an isotope assay, rather than a free continuing supply; an enriched or depleted wall requires its actual composition. `graphite_pool.py` keeps these balances explicit.

Annual treatment of a 50 µm skin handles **4.818×10¹⁰ kg/s** of matrix. Whole-process electrical costs of **2, 20 and 100 MJ/kg** require respectively **9.64×10¹⁶, 9.64×10¹⁷ and 4.82×10¹⁸ W**, or **0.012%, 0.120% and 0.601%** of that provisional export. A 100 µm skin doubles them. These costs are scenarios spanning selective or molecular processing through more intensive treatment; none requires atomizing the entire skin. New loads should be compared with the existing ordinary-auxiliary allowance before an additional debit is applied.

A molecular route can gasify carbon, separate the carbon and nitrogen streams, select isotope cuts, and redeposit carbon while recirculating H/O reagents in contained process cells. For the illustrative reaction `C(graphite) + H2O(g) → CO(g) + H2(g)`, NIST-JANAF formation values give **11.31 MJ/kg C reaction enthalpy** and **−0.640 MJ/kg C standard Gibbs energy at 1000 K**. The reverse reaction at the same standard activities has 0.640 MJ/kg positive free-energy demand, or 1.28 MJ/kg supplied work at the selected 50% conversion premise. Gas activities, kinetics, pressure work and heat integration set the actual process. This elementary reaction inventory does not establish a complete regeneration cost. [NIST-JANAF CO](https://janaf.nist.gov/tables/C-093.html), [water vapor](https://janaf.nist.gov/tables/H-064.html).

Nitrogen remains the distinct liberation task: N14/N15 implanted or chemically bound in graphite must be released into an assayed nitrogen stream. The graphite-pool substitution solves carbon identity and isotope dilution accounting; it does not remove nitrogen chemistry, capture of volatile products or the need for a suitable reusable surface.

## Liberation chemistry: what the primary evidence establishes

Aluminium carbide hydrolysis produces methane, and aluminium nitride hydrolysis produces ammonia. Closed hydrolysis therefore supplies chemical routes for separating C/N from an aluminium matrix. A carbide experiment obtained its reported complete analytical recovery after approximately 160–240 minutes at 60–80°C; its measured 90–91% recovery must not be promoted into either a universal conversion ceiling or a reactor loss fraction. Nitride experiments and industrial dross studies likewise show chemical release, while passivating reaction products and kinetics remain relevant. These experiments do not measure fuel liberation from the proposed irradiated skin. [Carbide experiment](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4532/3/1/8), [nitride hydrolysis experiments](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0009250923009624), [salt-cake gas measurements](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7433805/).

Liquid tin experiments demonstrate separation of carbon made during methane pyrolysis; the collected carbon can contain tin. This supports a chemically distinct catcher option, but the experiments use hotter bubble reactors and freshly formed carbon rather than dilute implanted C/N at the receiver's conditions. Tin inventory and gas containment must also be supplied. The current comparison therefore retains abundant graphite/alumina and contained aluminium options. [Geißler et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cej.2016.04.066), [Msheik et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhydene.2023.08.068).

## Finite isotope purity

The passive tracer calculation follows C12, C13, N14 and N15 individually through the saved temperature, density and light-particle backgrounds of each original pulse. Most mis-routed nuclei stay within the reusable quartet. N15 sent through A/B2/C13 has a nonquartet probability about **1.3–1.8×10⁻⁴ per tracer nucleus**; wrong C12/C13 in the nitrogen stages has much smaller nuclear leakage. Thus one percent contamination does not imply one percent catalyst destruction. Carbon coatings around N-bearing source material should be tested as actual recipes, with their proton competition and energy feedback included.

The interpolation qualification is material: native-isotope hot-output reconstruction differs from the saved hydro by L1 about **0.12% B2, 0.33% A, 1.09% B1 and 1.62% C13** per original catalyst nucleus. Four-to-eight midpoint subdivisions change the dominant probabilities by at most about 2×10⁻⁴; sparse original time sampling limits the remaining accuracy. Trace neutron/alpha consumption is a passive-background diagnostic, not an available infinite physical reservoir. Setting the fixed neutron background to zero changes every cold tracer probability by less than **7.8×10⁻⁷**, with the largest response in C13; this is below the dominant time-interpolation uncertainty. The screen supports moderate finite purity studies, not a precise acceptable impurity specification. Results and independent comparisons are in `isotope-tracer-final4.json`, `isotope-tracer-final8.json`, `isotope-tracer-zero-neutron.json` and `isotope-tracer-validation.json`. No arbitrary 99.999% purity requirement is imposed.

Within that original-family screen, assigning **1% impurity by catalyst number** in every stage and charging every impurity the largest observed nonquartet probability adds about **2.54×10⁻⁵ to fresh C:H**. This spends only **0.49% of the remaining .0056 fuel-carbon allowance**. It is a deliberately conservative comparison within the fixed-history model, rather than a physical upper bound after hydro feedback. The more relevant next purity test is the effect on useful reaction routing, proton availability and preparation, rather than catalyst destruction alone.

At 300 K the original stage-preserved atomic-mixture isotope separation free-energy floor is about **2.22×10¹² W**; separating all species has a floor about **1.41×10¹³ W**. Actual molecular chemistry, contact losses and machinery add costs, but the ideal separation work itself is far below station power. Gas-centrifuge calculations have explicitly treated C13 and N15 in several molecular forms; their equilibrium factors do not determine this installation's throughput. [Borisevich et al., 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-9002(00)00266-7).

## Decay storage and the optional oxygen loop

Using the same nuclear rates as the frozen pulses, additional hold times to reduce an existing parent population to 10⁻⁵ are about **19.5 minutes O14, 33.8 minutes O15 and 2.76 hours N13**. Three hours leaves approximately 3.66×10⁻⁶ of N13. Wall arrival already includes significant decay. Updated evaluated half-lives differ slightly from the inherited rates and do not alter this engineering timescale. Long C/N storage exceeds these waits automatically; the decay heat is produced near capture time and must be routed according to where it is deposited. [TUNL evaluated half-lives](https://nucldata.tunl.duke.edu/nucldata/HalfLife.shtml).

A possible nuclear return route for held O16 is `O16(p,gamma)F17; F17 beta+→O17; O17(p,alpha)N14`. Its net atomic-mass reaction is O16+2p→N14+He4, with **4.5526 MeV** before neutrino accounting. Restoring every current O16 side product would handle only .00012108 nuclei per existing net He. No preparation, apparatus or energy-recovery credit is taken without a separate rate and pulse design. At .0003607 fresh C:H, the existing process already fits the .007 available supply comfortably; an oxygen stage is an economic option, not a prerequisite.

## Priorities and uncertainty

The next receiver decisions concern prompt H/He capture, retention/release from a reusable skin, and the local regeneration/transfer geometry. There is a substantial chance that one of those changes the catcher architecture. By contrast, the current evidence does not justify extreme isotope-purity requirements or treating incomplete extraction as permanent loss. The 30–365 day batch range is an explicit design scan rather than an 80% credible interval: implanted-fuel release kinetics and skin damage have not yet calibrated a defensible residence-time posterior. The new mixture grid should be passed through the same eventual-return and reservoir accounting before choosing a final operating point.
