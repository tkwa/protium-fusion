# Alternative energy and fuel comparisons ✨

These order-of-magnitude comparisons help identify where an ordinary-hydrogen fusion plant might be useful. They are resource and energy scenarios, not full cost or market models. The [economics evaluator](README.md) carries the current plant's mass, output and manufacturing assumptions. Earlier detailed calculations and their source records remain in [v0.1](https://github.com/tkwa/protium-fusion/blob/v0.1/docs/protium-fusion/ECONOMICS.md); their old plant-specific examples are not current performance figures.

## Solar collection

For solar flux 1,361 W/m² at 1 AU, effective collector mass per area Σ and net conversion efficiency η, the specific power at distance r in AU is η × 1,361/(Σ r²) W/kg. With η=0.30 and Σ=1 kg/m², matching the current warm-buffer reference of 20.6576 mW/kg occurs near 141 AU. This example needs equal-service accounting for storage, intermittency, power distribution, equipment lifetime and location before it can support an economic choice.

## Deuterium in a hydrogen supply

The [Galileo Jupiter measurement](https://websites.umich.edu/~atreya/Articles/1998_Galileo_Probe_Measurements.pdf) reports D/H=(2.6±0.7)×10⁻⁵ by number for the sampled atmosphere. Actual abundance depends on the source. An earlier catalyzed-D–D scenario uses 3.453×10¹⁴ J per kg of deuterium and an assumed 30% net conversion; at the central D/H and complete recovery this gives approximately 5.4 GJ per kg of hydrogen feed. The calculation is a fuel-energy benchmark, not a demonstrated plant efficiency. Recovery, separation, equipment, delivery and finite plant lifetime must be included.

The ideal reversible separation work for atomic fractions x and 1−x at temperature T is −kT[x ln x+(1−x) ln(1−x)] per atom. This is a thermodynamic lower bound; actual molecular processing, incomplete recovery, cooling and equipment add costs. Source location can dominate: locally separating deuterium before transport is a different case from lifting all feed gas.

## Construction and other supply options

At full utilization, simple energy payback per installed kilogram is embodied energy divided by net specific output. At 20.6576 mW/kg, each 1 MJ/kg of embodied construction energy takes about 1.53 years of net output to repay; capacity utilization, replacement and financing alter the result. For any exported intermediate fuel, remove its associated energy from the electrical account. Black-hole accretion or other alternative energy sources require their own seed, equipment and resource accounts; an assumed conversion yield alone does not compare complete infrastructure.
