# Final350 gas inventory, pellet exposure and extraction interfaces ✨

The exact final350 cycle returns **3.227040 million kg/s of unburned hydrogen** and produces **2.758883 million kg/s of helium**. Gas enthalpy and ideal compression work are small on this station's scale. The consequential requirements are keeping released gas out of the delivery cavity, providing a real cleanup path for the fraction that reaches it, maintaining the irradiated front barrier, and controlling optical heat entering the pumps.

The baseline responses first assume all gas becomes1000K H₂+He. Release fractions, delayed populations and removal probabilities are explicit inputs. Prompt reflected energetic atoms, ions and secondary electrons remain separate populations; they do not instantly become this thermal gas. [Hydrogen evidence](../hydrogen-evidence/README.md) and [helium evidence](../helium-evidence/README.md) provide conditional release kernels without selecting a universal mobile fraction.

For a diffuse gas, the molecular wall flux gives `dM/dt=f Q(t)−g A vbar M/(4V)`, with `V=AR/3`. Here `g` includes geometric access, transmission and net removal into a return line. A hot wall bounce is not removal. At unit capture, residence times are452,535s for H₂ and637,699s for He. Full steady injection gives radial column6.35242×10⁻⁷kg/m² and pressure1.73596×10⁻⁹Pa. These formulas follow [molecular vacuum transport](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.0960); pressure alone is not a pellet acceptance criterion.

`chamber.py` resolves actual arrival quadratures and chronological sources. It compares prompt, one-hour, one-day and30-day material-release times and effective capture1,0.1 and0.01. Initial chamber/release reservoirs represent the corresponding steady source before the schedule starts. A low capture probability is not made favorable by starting an empty chamber.

A separate collisionless first-flight calculation uses diffuse cosine directions and flux-weighted Maxwellian speeds, spherical chord trajectories, and removal on first wall return. For dimensionless age `z=t sqrt(2kT/m)/R`, its survival is `S(z)=1−z²/2+(1+z²/2)exp(−4/z²)`. The radial-column kernel integrates inverse squared radius along each surviving cohort; its time integral reproduces the steady uniform result. A separate kinetic-energy survival kernel supplies the volume-averaged trace of the stress tensor. The pressure is not a local plume pressure.

Under full prompt release, the actual schedule gives a diffuse-balance peak column about8.85×10⁻⁷kg/m². The spherical first-flight peak is about8.48×10⁻⁷kg/m², with year mean6.37×10⁻⁷kg/m². The first-flight response is conditional on collisionless gas. The new `collision-screen.json` shows that the actual 100 nm routing case at global capture `g=0.01` is transitional across the reactor: a specified effective pair area of 10⁻¹⁹ m² gives H₂/He mean free paths 0.606/0.505 Gm, or Knudsen numbers 0.55/0.46. A primary scale check comes from [Hurly and Moldover’s helium calculations](https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.105.054): their 1000 K viscosity corresponds to a hard-sphere viscosity-equivalent area 1.14×10⁻¹⁹ m². That conversion is a transport scale, not the actual H₂–He scattering kernel. The packet varies the effective area from 10⁻²⁰ to 10⁻¹⁸ m². Gas collisions can change transient peaks and plume focusing. The uniform Maxwellian steady wall balance remains valid with intermolecular collisions; its actual g=0.01 reservoir varies little over this schedule. Neither treatment bounds a directed plume.

Arrival bins are causal: outgoing gas cannot appear in its own earlier incoming flight. Halving the150–300s grid spacing and doubling the64–128 arrival quadrature changes the reported encounter columns by less than0.05%. The first, noncausal linear-binning diagnostic was rejected; it is preserved in the raw work directory rather than used in this packet. `validation.json` records 71 conservation, kernel and refinement checks; joint-port resistance identities and routed-data closure are checked separately.

## Continuous local extraction and cold stages

The candidate geometry supplies150µm aligned material,100nm buried slits at20µm pitch, a50µm plenum with25% posts, and0.5mm-radius vias at1cm pitch through the backing. A hydraulic-diameter long-channel estimate gives effective slit/via series area7.739×10⁻⁶ of the receiver. Actual Clausing transport, bends and manifold restrictions remain to be calculated. The50µm geometric plenum has37.5µm void-equivalent depth; slit and via storage depends on valve location and temperature.

With a downstream pump speed of0.001m³/s per receiver m² for each species, the combined channel/pump conductance gives mean source pressure0.001477Pa and prompt-thermalized upper source peaks4.58Pa(A),0.0518Pa(B1),3.27Pa(B2) and2.34Pa(C13). Delayed release smooths those peaks. Full-shot storage instead reaches much higher pressure; the separate void-volume table makes that dependence explicit.

The concrete sequence is a retained collection surface and sealed side channels, a contaminant-management stage, parallel regenerable H getters and He sorbers, then isolated regeneration/compression into the fuel/product inventory. H getters dissociate/dissolve hydrogen and are regenerated under pumping; noble helium needs its own path. TiZrV activation and performance are supported by [primary getter measurements](https://cds.cern.ch/record/512680). Hydrocarbons, nitrogen and surface blocking require chemical separation or staged pumping; pure-H speed is not automatically mixed-gas speed.

For an explicit equipment scale, a selected H-getter speed10m³/s/m² at300K and the measured low-load He cryopanel graph scale13m³/s/m² at273.15K correspond to active areas5.477×10⁻⁵ and4.020×10⁻⁵ of the receiver for that0.001m/s downstream speed. The helium source uses5–7K charcoal, with strong temperature/loading dependence; the reported loaded6.5K equilibrium near10⁻⁶Pa must enter backstreaming. It is not a zero-pressure sink. Regeneration around85K is isolated from the chamber. [Day et al. primary cryopanel programme](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/servlets/purl/20047879).

The original equal-active-area/equal-optical-view comparison gives 5.385 W/m², but does not close the admission geometry. `joint-ports.json` replaces that handoff with a shared mouth occupying **10⁻⁴ m² per receiver m²**, net molecular transmission **0.1**, and the same target downstream speed **0.001 m³/s/m² per species**. Molecular admission and active pumping resistances are in series: `C=a_mouth tau vbar/4`, `1/S=1/C+1/(a_panel S_panel)`. Required H-getter and He-sorber active fractions are **6.248×10⁻⁵** and **4.867×10⁻⁵**. Day’s quoted speed is normalized to panel area; it supplies no credit for this new baffle path.

Charging every 1000 K photon crossing the sealed mouth to the colder system gives a conservative aperture heat limit **5.67037 W/m²**. This is tied to the same gas mouth and counts only once as hot-to-cold bypass. A 300 K first intercept/getter and a 200 K helium shield precede isolated 5 K sorbers. Full 200 K radiative view over the He active area requires **0.34440 W/m² electric** at 50% of Carnot performance. If 10⁻⁴ of admitted hot photon energy bypasses the intercepts and reaches 5 K, refrigeration adds **0.04423 W/m²**. ## Residual chamber gas, defects and plumes

An intact gas barrier also blocks ambient gas from re-entering through that face. Consequently the local collection surface does not provide a free global capture `g=0.01`. `pumping-interfaces.json` gives a coupled optical/gas frontier: atg=0.01, an unshielded cold aperture exposed to1000K radiance admits567W/m² before refrigeration. The gas transmission and optical coupling must be evaluated together. The separate [warm ion-cleanup study](../warm-ion-cleanup/README.md) provides an alternative physical ionization, field-confinement, implantation and regeneration route.

For the local0.001m/s pump example, a connected molecular-hole area around1.1×10⁻⁹(H₂) or1.4×10⁻⁹(He) of the receiver already leaks0.001 of the gas entering that plenum. Direct front release uses part of the total allowance first: `f_total=f_direct+(1−f_direct)f_plenum`. Solid H permeation, seams, irradiation defects and pump backpressure add separately. No pristine membrane transmission is assigned to the damaged hot catcher.

A directed plume can cross a packet even when average chamber pressure is low. The packet provides footprint-concentration examples and inward radial-flow examples with finite angular blur. For the latter, `rho=Q/(4 pi u r²)` and column scales as `Q/(4 pi u r_min)`; it can exceed the diffuse response substantially. Those are explicit angular scenarios, not inferred pore emission distributions. Dust is also separate: micron-scale impacts resemble distributed heating, while sparse larger grains can damage individual pellets despite a small average mass column. No dust production rate is invented.

The exact packet-weighted 100 nm-cap, 20 µm-pitch, anisotropy-10⁵ mobile routing produces front fractions **0.001030723 for H** and **0.000875485 for He**, corresponding to **3326.18 and 2415.36 kg/s**. It excludes added reflection, trapped populations, front permeation and backstreaming. With independently supplied global capture `g=0.01`, the actual chronological diffuse calculation gives mean column **6.0163×10⁻⁸ kg/m²**, peak **6.0327×10⁻⁸ kg/m²**, mean pressure **1.6904×10⁻¹⁰ Pa**, and peak **1.6965×10⁻¹⁰ Pa**. The 10 nm comparison gives a mean column **1.2219×10⁻⁸ kg/m²** under the same conditional capture.

`routed-pellets.json` applies these packet-specific arrivals directly to the capsule model. For the 100 nm/g=0.01 case, a 1 cm, 80%-filled C13 grain at median incoming speed receives 13.59 kJ/kg from gas, bringing IR+field+gas to **258.60 kJ/kg**. That lies within the higher of the two 251–282 kJ/kg material enthalpy cases. The 99.9%-speed grain receives 48.09 kJ/kg gas and totals **186.02 kJ/kg**. The 10th-speed-percentile 1 cm grain already exceeds the solid-core comparison from IR; changing the gas model does not resolve that delivery requirement. At 2 cm, the corresponding 10th/median/99.9%-speed totals are **199.06/137.50/101.22 kJ/kg**, with the separate source-hardware consequences above.

The median 1 cm grain’s geometric uniform-drag delay is **1.39 µs**; its near-launch-layer limit is **2.78 µs**. These are repeatable mean shifts in the stated model. Differential spatial gas columns, size dispersion, local plumes and ejecta recoil determine uncompensated timing and pointing errors. No assembly acceptance is inferred from average pressure.

`route-inputs.json` preserves the parent's packet-specific finite-cap routing arrays and exact source counts. `route_response.py` propagates those already-mobile ideal routing cases through this chronological calculation, keeping reflection, trapped populations, permeability and backpressure outside the selected fraction. The source solver has tiny negative exit-probability roundoff, as low as −6.8×10⁻¹². Only the auxiliary gas emission weights are projected to zero; original probability arrays and hydro abundances remain unchanged, with per-stage mass corrections recorded. The largest material correction is below a kilogram in a trillion-kilogram pulse. `routing-validation.json` passes 72 checks, including an independent 300→150 s time-grid comparison; the chosen g=0.01 encounter changes by about two parts per million. Heating or ionization never removes matter by itself; only transport into a retained return/product reservoir receives capture credit.

## Chemical gas forms and the nitrogen threshold

`chemistry.json` measures chemical-form changes against a **10% increase in the total steady residual-gas mass column**. This is an explicit comparison criterion; pellet acceptance still depends on the source trajectory and skin response. Keeping the escaped hydrogen nuclei fixed while forming CH₄ or NH₃ adds C/N mass and lowers thermal speed. At the same global capture `g=0.01`, the fraction of escaped H nuclei that raises the total column by 10% is **1.981% for C12H4**, **1.793% for C13H4**, **1.317% for N14H3**, or **1.202% for N15H3**. The table varies species capture from 0.001 to 0.1. It also separates the chemical fraction of all returned hydrogen from the molecular branch’s cavity-entry fraction; those are different parameters.

The actual material cycle returns **24.4528 million kg/s of nitrogen after decays**. If a fraction enters the cavity as N₂, the corresponding threshold is only **7.2506 ppm of nitrogen return**, or **177.30 kg/s**, at `g_N2=0.01`. Capture 0.001/0.1 changes that threshold to 0.7251/72.506 ppm. This fraction combines chemical liberation and transport through the front boundary. Nitrogen retained in the catcher or processed through sealed channels does not contribute to cavity gas.

The H release evidence includes molecular hydrogen and hydrocarbon signals but does not establish these reactor chemical fractions. Warm process filters can manage compounds inside the closed channel system; molecules already emitted into the cavity need their own capture and transfer path. Carbon or nitrogen carried by these gases remains station inventory unless it actually escapes. No fresh-material loss is assigned from this gas-column sensitivity.
