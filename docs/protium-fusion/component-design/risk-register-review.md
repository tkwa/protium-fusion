# ✨ Independent design-change priority review

Updated checkpoint, 2026-10-31. The prioritization event is a **substantial architecture or operating-point change**: a different delivery/collector system, a serious radius/cadence/mass change, loss of the carbon-feed budget, or failure to deliver the required plant-specific power. A parameter moving by 10% does not by itself qualify. The probabilities below are subjective research priorities conditional on the current reference, not measured failure rates or probabilities that an advanced civilization cannot solve the problem.

| Consequence event | Current evidence | Subjective chance / action |
|---|---|---|
| Full-cavity 0.1–1 mm cold grains need a protected path, larger grains or substantially improved optical coating | Primary Al optical integration gives 1 mm polymer only 73–81 s of accepted exposure versus 458 s flight; 10 mm passes the bulk-dose screen | **Above 90%** for ordinary Al-coated grains at the stated speed, wall temperature and acceptance. Already requires an explicit delivery architecture; prioritize now. |
| Receiver collection material changes because refractory-carbon contact destroys or dilutes recovered isotope stocks | Ion/stopping agent is computing nuclear and chemical consequences of carbon, hydrogen-rich braking and metal capture | **Above 10%**; strong mechanistic reason, outcome pending that calculation. A local recoverable metal layer is a concrete alternative. |

High-Z tin increases shallow absorption; aluminum is a separate material case | **Above 10%** until the spectrum-dependent surface-temperature and vapor-dose screen is complete. Compute that screen rather than assume the whole layer absorbs uniformly. |

Spectrum can materially affect a newly chosen thin surface and cold fuel exposure. |

Useful uncertainty reporting should separate deterministic acceptance from unknown achieved performance. For example, the required 1 mm polymer absorptivity is computed from specified calorimetry, geometry and temperature endpoints; the achieved absorptivity of a manufactured cold charged grain is an unresolved material property. Assigning it a narrow numerical 80% interval before choosing its layers would imply evidence we do not have. The radiation-interface memo now adds a broad conditional Al-coating absorptivity judgment, central 0.022 and 80% interval 0.007–0.06, and its derived maximum-free-flight interval. This is an engineering prior for a specified surface class; measured coating response should replace it.


Completed discriminators update the priorities:

- **Exposed pure molten Al requires local containment or another front surface:** above 99% conditional on a 1000 K pure-metal front and the stated cold-grain heat allocation. JANAF vapor pressure establishes the contamination requirement. Gross circulation remains recoverable matter.
- **Thermal oxide vapor alone requires a larger radius:** below 10% conditional on the original separated temperature histories plus 10 K contact allowance. The original equilibrium source is about 69 kg/s, with isolated maximum column heating only about 3.5 J/kg for 1 cm grains. The new richer spectra have higher material flux and can need about 15 K contact allowance at 50 μm thickness and k=5 W/m/K; this original result should not be relabeled as a completed new-mixture vapor calculation.
- **A local prompt H/He return interface is needed:** above 90% for the 1000 K front and sources in the reference station. The 1/10/100 kJ/kg gas allocations give finite targets rather than an arbitrary universal failure threshold. At the 10 kJ/kg, 1 cm benchmark, common chamber injection/effective capture must stay below 0.110 in the reference or 0.087 in the richer A-H2/B1-H2 case. A designed local backplenum can satisfy the geometry without global cryocondensation. Its actual mass/power remains to be established. The later provisional A3/B1H2/B2H2.5/C13H4 point gives 0.06335 at the same benchmark; all three heat allocations are saved in the return-gas results.
- **Opacity-dependent reruns change a preferred hot mixture or receiver load:** above 10%, with a broad subjective central chance around 40% before the reruns. The sampled primary Compton scale is 6–21 times the old grey value. Completed C13 comparisons already show roughly tenfold reductions in escaped photon energy at opacity 0.01. This event is a design-choice change, not a probability of reactor failure.
- **An H-rich hot mixture needs a different cold-feed arrangement:** above 50% if substantial separate condensed H₂ is required. CH₂/CH₄ or other condensed hydrocarbon recipes can avoid that particular constraint for H:C 2–4. Their pressure, density and infrared exposure must be evaluated rather than imposing the CH₂+H₂ burden as a universal minimum.
- **A thin oxide rather than graphite is selected:** still unresolved. The harder and softer photospheric proxies give materially different oxide flashes, while both finite-depth carbon proxies fit inside the previous conservative carbon thermal envelope for the selected C13 examples. Graphite isotope recovery and oxide stopping/chemical return are competing whole-loop choices.
- **Nonideal EOS terms force a major change in a final contender:** above 10% near a sharp ignition threshold is a reasonable research priority, without a supported narrow numerical probability. The ion-sphere diagnostic is below 1% of total initial prepared energy but up to 22% of local initial pressure. A consistent finalist sensitivity is justified; a complete grid rerun solely from the coupling parameter is not.

These subjective probabilities allocate research effort. They are not independent and should not be multiplied into a reactor success probability. Conditional optical absorptivity retains the stated central 0.022 and 80% interval 0.007–0.06; unchosen trap efficiencies and spectra are represented by acceptance curves and explicit alternatives rather than unsupported narrow intervals.
