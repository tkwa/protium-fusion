# Receiver carbon survival after electromagnetic braking

The selected process reserves a fresh structural-carbon mass equal to 0.0014 of fresh hydrogen. Its circulating inventory gives **0.000470838 carbon atoms per net helium atom** for all irreversible material losses, compared with 7.58694 incident nuclei across all passes. If this whole allowance went to the receiver, its mean irreversible carbon loss could be at most **0.0000620591 atom per incident nucleus**. Other carbon hardware consumes part of the same allowance. These are inventory constraints independently calculated by [wall_stopping_requirement.py](wall_stopping_requirement.py), with inputs and results in [wall-stopping-requirement.json](wall-stopping-requirement.json).

There is no common positive reaction threshold below which all damaging channels disappear. The mass-table calculation in [stopping-channel-thresholds.json](../open-loop-redesign/stopping-channel-thresholds.json) gives positive Q values for proton radiative capture on both carbon isotopes, carbon fusion branches, and carbon-13(alpha,neutron)oxygen-16. Primary LUNA measurements observe proton captures on carbon down to 60 keV center-of-mass energy and the carbon-13 alpha-neutron reaction at 230–300 keV center-of-mass energy. These data establish the open channels; they do not determine a reactor wall's integrated destruction probability. Sources: [Skowronski et al., PRL131,162701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16098), [Ciani et al., PRL127,152701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00303).

A **1 keV per atomic-mass-unit wall-entry ceiling** is a concrete candidate qualification for the charged debris. Across the calculated 71.3638 baryons in all incident passes per net helium, that limits residual directed kinetic energy to 0.0713638 MeV per helium. It requires removing at least **99.6416% of the calculated bulk kinetic energy** before impact. The removed energy must enter captured electrical loads, fields, or coolant. The selected 99% total energy-collection fraction would still need to include the losses of this braking system. A static magnetic field alone does no work; energy extraction requires electric fields, including fields induced by time-dependent magnetic circuits. No decelerator efficiency, mass, charge-state acceptance, or transient-force design is derived here.

At 1 keV/u, the bare Coulomb tunneling exponent on carbon is approximately 187.7 for protons, 375.5 for alpha particles, 1126 for carbon, and 1314 for nitrogen. This puts charged reactions deeply below the Coulomb barrier. The calculation uses the conventional astrophysical form sigma=S(E)/E times exp(-2 pi eta), with 2 pi eta=31.29 Z1 Z2 sqrt(mu/E), where mu is in atomic mass units and center-of-mass E is in keV. For a projectile of mass A at e keV/u on a stationary target, mu/E=1/e in the mass-number approximation. S factors, resonances, electronic screening and the actual energy distribution still enter; the exponent alone is **not a certified cross-section or loss bound**. See the formula and extrapolation discussion in [Bemmerer et al., Nuclear Physics A779,297–317](https://archive.int.washington.edu/PROGRAMS/solar_papers/14N%2Bp/bemmerer.pdf).

The uncontrolled tail is potentially more restrictive than the nominal entry energy. If a hot-tail projectile irreversibly destroys an average Y carbon atoms, its fraction of the total incident population must be below 0.0000620591/Y, before allocating allowance to other losses. For example, Y=10 would require a fraction below 0.00000620591. Values of Y here are illustrative variables, not stopping predictions. Neutral energetic debris, escaped charged particles, secondary neutrons and energetic photons require their own transport accounting; an electromagnetic energy ceiling cannot be applied to neutral radiation.

Reaction bookkeeping must follow the final recovered material. Carbon-12 proton capture forms nitrogen-13, which decays back to carbon-13; this activation alone is not permanent elemental-carbon loss. Nitrogen and oxygen products may also be returned through additional processing if that processing and its energy costs are explicitly included.

The next decisive calculation is a species- and spectrum-resolved stopping integral: integrate target-carbon destruction cross sections along each slowing trajectory, then count permanent transmutation and unrecycled ejection. It needs measured or defensibly modeled charge states and high-energy tails, target isotopic composition, stopping powers, reaction channels and material recovery. [NIST PSTAR and ASTAR](https://www.nist.gov/pml/stopping-power-range-tables-electrons-protons-and-helium-ions) provide stopping/range data for protons and helium ions; a heavy-ion and radiation treatment must be added. The term nuclear stopping in such tables means elastic energy transfer to target nuclei and does not automatically imply transmutation.

No fundamental contradiction with the carbon allowance follows from the charged-particle bookkeeping: sufficiently slow charged debris can have very small nuclear reaction probabilities, and its extracted energy can remain inside the plant. The bookkeeping also does not establish that the declared braking hardware and material-recovery system attain the allowance. A history that selects successful operation should describe measured particle entry spectra and integrated irreversible carbon loss, rather than claim that every reaction was closed by a threshold.
