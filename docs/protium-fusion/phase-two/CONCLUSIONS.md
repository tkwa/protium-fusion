# Second-phase conclusions ✨

The investigation supports two physically motivated experimental routes, with substantial unresolved engineering between their component calculations and a power station. It establishes neither a complete reactor achieving 1 mW/kg nor a minimum mass of one Jupiter for every possible engineered hydrogen-burning system. The selected 2047–2049 history therefore deploys experimental installations and leaves the complete plants as proposals.

## Opaque catalytic pulses

The hot interiors of the saved compressed pulses absorb and redistribute radiation effectively. The separate scattering and absorption audit also supports the smaller nitrogen experiment: its first-percentile effective cell thermalization depth is about 17,000, and remains about 170 when both prescribed scattering and absorption coefficients are reduced by a factor of 100. Bremsstrahlung energy can remain in an opaque assembly. Radiation pressure, expansion and the energy needed to assemble that state remain part of its dynamics. See [pulse transport](pulse-transport/README.md).

A completed favorable hot-inflow calculation for a 30 m reference radius contains 1.13×10¹³ kg, receives 3.49×10²⁵ J and releases 2.86×10²⁶ J in its nitrogen capture stage, giving Q=8.182. The radius is a compressed reference coordinate, not the outer radius of the entire pulse. This is a prescribed incoming state. It supplies no demonstration that a driver can deliver that state from cold parcels.

The explicit [driver audit](driver/REPORT.md) identifies acceleration length, electric stress, heating, neutralization, pointing and merger entropy as coupled requirements. In the illustrative charged-grain small pulse, uncompensated collective electrostatic energy becomes comparable to the incoming kinetic energy around 175 km radius. The stated electrostatic penalty allowance requires approximately 99.87% compensation. Electron mass is small; delivery and phase-space preservation still need apparatus.

Cold-inflow simulations cannot yet assign a trustworthy completed gain. Coarse cases underburn; refined cases compress much harder, reach the 2.8 GK guard, and depend strongly on artificial viscosity. Partial reaction energy at that guard is not a final energy yield. The surrogate fully ionized cold EOS also needs replacement. [COLD-COMPRESSION.md](driver/COLD-COMPRESSION.md) preserves failures as well as successful diagnostics.

For the article, Q=2.6 is an explicitly selected fictional capture-stage result below the favorable hot reference. At 30% driver efficiency and 40% thermal conversion, even complete collection returns only 43.2% of the source electricity, counting deposited driver energy once. Break-even would require Q>7.333 before auxiliaries. A range radius of 10⁹ m gives about 10 MJ/m² of immediate input-plus-nuclear energy averaged over a sphere. Shielding and actual spectral loads remain design requirements. Subsequent decay, recovery and other catalytic stages are still required to burn hydrogen fully to helium.

## Electron-poor ion channels

The [driven-ion calculation](ion-dynamics/README.md) replaces an invalid preliminary response with a passive orbit-Fourier electrostatic response. A fast axial wave can still heat radial ion motion. In the chosen geometry, a 1 kV wall wave at 0.1c dissipates about 51.5 kW per active metre while maintained. Useful heat may replace an existing heater load; it must not automatically be charged again as an additional electrical loss. Finite lepton capture, chirped acceleration, end fields and nonlinear evolution remain unvalidated. Positrons must also cross or bypass the ion-retaining end potential.

The [gas calculation](gas-supply/REPORT.md) finds approximately 30% first-flight proton ionization of diffuse incoming room-temperature H₂, with an independent deterministic chord calculation agreeing with the stochastic screen. Repeated wall flights raise eventual ionization when sticking is weak. This explains why pumping capacity alone does not determine the electron source. Actual irradiated outgassing, secondary production, chemistry and a complete charge/energy circuit still require material and apparatus information. Birth potential alone is not a net energy debit.

[Periodic discharge](periodic-reset/PERIODIC-RESET.md) is a concrete experimental mode. Its account preserves the energized solenoid and distinguishes random thermal energy from recoverable rotation and self-electric fields. Across the specified prompt-pair and per-positron loss scenarios, no separated-efficiency case reaches the 1 mW/kg target. The favorable case gives 0.994 TW from 1.505×10¹⁵ kg, or 0.661 mW/kg, with a 2,167 s burn. It still needs isotope extraction during that burn. Millisecond pilot shots incur much larger preparation costs per reaction. These results constrain the specified reset scheme, rather than all possible clearing designs.

## Complete-plant consequences

The [plant audit](plant-audit/README.md) finds only 0.2107 percentage points of neutron-loss allowance remaining after bare carbon and water oxygen in its common-flux screen, if the old 99.5% useful-capture assumption is retained. That allowance permits approximately 0.60 μm of titanium or 0.93 μm of the specified Ti/Zr/V getter, before other losses. A spatial neutron transport calculation is needed to choose actual materials and geometry.

The nominal ion engine processes 14.129 TW including recovered circulation losses. Explicit heat-routing designs consume roughly 10¹⁴ kg and tens to hundreds of gigawatts. A warm-hydrogen, radiator-local engine example adds 1.295×10¹⁴ kg and about 100 GW; charging it entirely additionally reduces the old reference to 1.263 mW/kg, before the unresolved particle-clearing costs. Better temperature headroom can make central engines competitive. Existing allowances must be credited before charging duplicated equipment.

The first-phase [economics](../ECONOMICS.md) remains consequential: construction-energy repayment takes millennia at these specific powers, replacement can substantially raise throughput, and deuterium extraction and sunlight offer strong alternatives. Building an eleven-Earth-mass opaque plant also requires a separate enormous material campaign. These results motivate long-lived isolated applications while supporting modest scientific pilots in the selected 2040s history.

Future computation can address multidimensional parcel merger with a cold-material EOS, nonlinear electromagnetic extraction with boundaries, and coupled neutron/thermal/structural layouts. Experiments remain necessary to establish surface gas yields, damage, combined material properties, finite capture and recovery, and integrated driver performance. The present work is sufficient to select and explain the article’s pilot outcomes without assigning those unknowns fictitious real-world solutions.
